Solid oxide fuel cells (SOFCs) allow us to directly convert the chemical energy of fuel into electricity. Their emissions consist mainly of H2O and CO2, and their efficiency can reach 70%.
In chemistry, water is written as H2O; in mathematics, a square can be written as x2. The mixture of CO and H2 (synthesis gas) supplied to the SOFC anode can be produced from natural gas using dry or steam reforming at 650–850oC.
